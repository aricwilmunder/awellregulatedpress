Interpretation

Historical interpretation begins with evidence and asks how that evidence can help people understand the lives, places, objects, and experiences of the past.

At A Well-Regulated Press, interpretation grows from preservation and research. Surviving artifacts, documents, architecture, clothing, photographs, and other primary sources provide the constraints. Historical interpretation then brings those sources together in ways that can make the past more understandable, accessible, and engaging.

Historic Reimaginings

Historic Reimaginings is a research-driven process for developing historically informed visual interpretations. The work begins with artifacts, archival records, architecture, clothing, photographs, and other period sources, establishing what is known, what is uncertain, and what requires further investigation.

Interpretations are developed iteratively, reviewed against the evidence, corrected, and refined. AI-assisted visualization can accelerate that process, but historical judgment and review remain human.

Evidence First

Interpretation does not replace the evidence. It uses evidence to explore how people may have experienced the objects, places, and moments that survive.

Preservation protects the evidence. Research establishes what we know. Interpretation asks how that evidence can help us understand the past.
