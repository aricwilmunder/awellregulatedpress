Community

Historical work is strengthened by the people and communities connected to it.

A Well-Regulated Press works with historical organizations, museums, educators, researchers, specialists, descendants, and local communities to develop, review, preserve, and share historically grounded work.

Lincoln County Historical Association

The work surrounding the Prescott family and Pownalborough Court House has developed through an ongoing relationship with the Lincoln County Historical Association in Maine.

Access to the historic site and collections, institutional knowledge, review, discussion, educational work, public programs, and the return of the Prescott family samplers have all helped shape the projects.

Visit the Lincoln County Historical Association

Museums, Archives & Collections

Historical work grows through relationships with the institutions and specialists who preserve and interpret surviving evidence. Research for A Well-Regulated Press projects has included work with collections and experts at museums, archives, historic sites, and universities across the country, providing access to artifacts, clothing, documents, architecture, and specialized knowledge that help place historical evidence in context.

Research & Expert Review

Projects are strengthened through conversations with historians, curators, archivists, educators, subject specialists, and others who can identify problems, suggest sources, challenge assumptions, and improve the final work.

Returning History Home

Community can also mean recognizing where an artifact or historical record belongs. Returning the Prescott family samplers to Pownalborough and a 1928 Weldon Spring advertising calendar to the community where it originated are examples of stewardship that restores historical context as well as preserving the object.

Shared History

Books, research, educational materials, and public programs create opportunities for descendants, local residents, readers, educators, and historical organizations to contribute knowledge, ask new questions, and build continuing relationships around the past.

History Through Relationships

Community is not simply an audience for historical work. Relationships can shape the questions asked, the evidence discovered, the interpretations developed, and the ways history is preserved and shared.

Education creates opportunities to learn from history. Outreach carries that work into wider public settings.
